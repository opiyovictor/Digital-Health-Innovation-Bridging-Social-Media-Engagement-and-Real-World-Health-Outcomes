# Executive summary

## What the analysis shows

The survey suggests that Vijana Tubonge had strong reach among respondents who participated in the assessment, with **83.9% (318/379)** reporting awareness of the platform. Among those aware, **84.9% (270/318)** reported following the account.

Engagement was frequent: **38.1%** of followers reported watching content 3–5 times per week and **19.3%** reported watching daily. The strongest content interests were sexual and reproductive health (**65.9%**), HIV testing/PrEP/PEP (**64.8%**) and mental health (**56.3%**).

Perceptions of the platform were also positive. **91.9%** of followers were comfortable or very comfortable asking health questions, **96.3%** trusted the health information, and **95.6%** felt safe and respected.

On self-reported influence, **97.0%** agreed or strongly agreed that the platform increased their health knowledge. **95.6%** said content encouraged them to seek services when needed, and **95.6%** reported greater confidence in making health decisions.

The referral pathway provides a particularly useful operational insight: **110 of 270 followers (40.7%)** reported receiving a referral, and **106 of those 110 (96.4%)** reported accessing the referred service. All 106 respondents who reported accessing a referred service rated it as helpful or very helpful.

## What this does — and does not — establish

These findings are strong evidence of **reach, engagement and positive self-reported experiences**. They are not causal evidence that the TikTok intervention produced the observed changes. The study is cross-sectional and relies on self-report; the awareness-before/after comparison is retrospective.

That distinction is deliberate. The analytical goal is not to oversell the result, but to show how survey data can be translated into decisions about content strategy, digital engagement and referral pathways.
